cell cycle

Prophase (stage 1)

Prophase is the first and longest stage of mitosis, the process where a single cell divides into two identical daughter cells. During this critical phase, the loosely packed chromatin inside the nucleus condenses into highly visible, tightly coiled chromosomes, each consisting of two identical sister chromatids joined at a centromere. As this occurs, the nucleolus disappears, and the nuclear envelope begins to break down, allowing the cellular machinery access to the genetic material. Outside the nucleus, structures called centrosomes move toward opposite poles of the cell and begin radiating protein fibers to form the mitotic spindle. This spindle apparatus will ultimately attach to the chromosomes and orchestrate their movement, setting the stage for accurate genetic separation in the subsequent phases of cell division.
